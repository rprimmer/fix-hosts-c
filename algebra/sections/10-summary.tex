\section{Summary}

The central algebra is compact:

\begin{align*}
\Insert{d}(A)
  &= \begin{cases}
       A, & d\in\Lines(A),\\
       A\concat\langle d\rangle, & d\notin\Lines(A),
     \end{cases}\\[0.5em]
\Remove{d}(H)
  &= \mathsf{filter}(\text{lines not containing }d\text{ as an exact token},H),\\
\Add{d}(S)
  &= S[A\coloneqq\Insert{d}(S.A),
       B\coloneqq\Some{S.H},
       H\coloneqq\Remove{d}(S.H)],\\
\Prep_E(S)
  &= S[O\coloneqq\Some{S.H},H\coloneqq\HBlock(S.A,E)],\\
\Restore(S)
  &= S[H\coloneqq H_O]\quad\text{when }S.O=\Some{H_O},\\
\Flush(S)
  &= S[K\coloneqq\Fresh].
\end{align*}

Its principal laws are:

\begin{align*}
\Remove{d}\circ\Remove{d} &= \Remove{d},\\
\Remove{d}\circ\Remove{e} &= \Remove{e}\circ\Remove{d},\\
\Insert{d}\circ\Insert{d} &= \Insert{d},\\
\Add{d}(\Add{d}(S)) &\HAequiv\Add{d}(S),\\
\Restore(\Prep_E(S)).H &= S.H.
\end{align*}

Quiet and verbose forms produce the same modeled state while producing
different traces.

The most important qualifications are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Add is not fully idempotent when \path{hosts.bak} is observed.
  \item Distinct adds commute setwise, but not in file order or backup history.
  \item Prep is not idempotent because it advances the restoration point.
  \item Atomic rename protects one pathname, not the complete multi-file
        operation.
  \item Atomic visibility does not by itself imply maximum crash durability.
\end{itemize}

These qualifications are where the algebra adds the most value.  Familiar
operational claims become precise enough to verify, refute, or deliberately
adopt as part of the program's contract.
